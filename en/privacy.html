Diana privacy policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Diana is an iPhone app for practising English by talking out loud with a teacher, Diana, who is an artificial intelligence and gets to know you call by call. This policy explains what data Diana uses, what for, who processes it, how long it is kept and what you can do about it.

In short

  • Your voice is used while you talk and the audio is not stored.
  • Diana remembers what you tell her (your name, your life, your people, your plans, your projects, what worries you, your mistakes) to use it in later calls. It is stored on a server in Frankfurt (Germany).
  • To work, your voice goes to Deepgram (on its European Union server) and the text of the conversation goes to Anthropic, which stores it in the United States for up to 30 days.
  • No ads, no tracking across other apps and we don't sell your data. No service trains its models on your calls.
  • In Settings you can withdraw your consent, delete everything Diana knows about you, delete your account and request your data.
  • Diana is only for people 18 or older.

1. Who is responsible

The controller of your data is Juan Llorente Fontes, who develops Diana (Spain).

For anything about your data: soporte.juanllfontes@icloud.com.

2. What data is used, what for and for how long

Your account

You sign in with "Sign in with Apple". Diana does not ask Apple for your email or your name: it only receives an identifier for your account from Apple. With it, your account is created on the server and Diana remembers you even if you change iPhone. A code from Apple is also kept, which is used to revoke Diana's access to your Apple account when you delete it.

Legal basis: needed to provide the service (contract).

How long: until you delete your account.

Your sessions

Each time you sign in, the server records the internet address (IP) you connect from and a technical description of the app and the iPhone system using it. The server also keeps technical logs of the requests it receives, which may include the internet address; these logs do not include what you say to Diana.

Legal basis: legitimate interest in keeping the service working and secure.

How long: session data, while the session lasts (at most, until you delete your account); technical logs, a few days (7 at most).

Your voice

While you talk to Diana, your voice is sent to Deepgram, which turns it into text. Deepgram also gives Diana her voice from her text. The audio is not stored: not on the iPhone, not on Diana's server, and Deepgram only uses it while processing each request.

Legal basis: your consent, which you give in the privacy notice before your first call.

The conversation

During the call, the text of what you and Diana say is sent to Anthropic (Claude), which writes Diana's replies. Diana's instructions and what Diana remembers about you are sent with it. When you hang up, the conversation is sent to Anthropic again to write the call summary and update Diana's memory. When the call starts, what Diana remembers about you is sent to Anthropic once more, to choose what is worth talking about today. With each sentence, the app also sends the date and time on your iPhone to Diana's server, with its offset from UTC (not the name of your time zone or your location), and Anthropic receives the day and time, so that Diana knows what time it is; that time is not stored. The full text of the conversation is not stored on Diana's server: only the summary and the memory. The iPhone keeps it while the call lasts.

Legal basis: your consent (explicit consent, because you may share sensitive things, like your health).

How long: Anthropic deletes it within 30 days (see section 4).

Your call summaries

What you talked about, Diana's corrections and new words or phrases. You see them in History.

Legal basis: your consent.

How long: until you tap "Delete everything Diana knows about me" or delete your account.

Diana's memory

What Diana learns about you in calls: your name (she asks for it in the first call), things about your life and what you like; your habits (like padel on Wednesdays), your ongoing projects (like a renovation) and what worries you, to ask you how it is going; the people in your life (their name and who they are to you); your plans with a date, to ask you how they went; your frequent mistakes and your English level. For each thing she keeps what kind of thing it is, which people and other things it is linked to, and when you last talked about it. What ends (a finished project, something that got resolved) is not deleted straight away: it becomes part of your story. If something that worries you is serious (for example, a death or a crisis), Diana notes it so she can handle it with care. She also keeps how she started your last 5 calls, so as not to repeat herself. It may include personal things you share, like your health. There is no screen to see it: Diana uses it in calls, as a friend would.

Legal basis: your explicit consent.

How long: until you tap "Delete everything Diana knows about me" or delete your account. The memory has a maximum size: when it is full, Diana forgets the oldest things that are already over first.

Your minutes and your subscription

The minutes you have used, your plan, the call you have open and its pauses, and for your subscription: its number at Apple, the plan, when it expires and what you used that week. To check your purchases, the server asks Apple about that number. Payments are handled by Apple: Diana never sees your card or payment details.

Legal basis: needed to give you your plan's minutes (contract).

How long: until you delete your account (the open call, until you hang up), with the exception in section 6.

Your consent

When you accepted the privacy notice and when you withdrew it, with the notice version and its language.

Legal basis: legal obligation to be able to prove you gave your consent.

How long: until you delete your account.

Usage limits

To prevent abuse, the server counts how many requests each account makes (for example, how many replies from Diana per hour).

Legal basis: legitimate interest in protecting the service.

How long: requests, one hour. How long you've been paused or reconnecting that day (for the 15-minutes-a-day limit in the terms), until you delete your account; it starts again each day.

On your iPhone

The accent you choose for Diana, that you went past the welcome screen and accepted the notice, and your session pass (in the iPhone keychain). They stay on your iPhone. The accent and the welcome and notice flags are removed when you delete the app; the session pass stays in the keychain until the app ends your session: when you delete your account, or if Apple or the server stop accepting it.

3. What Diana doesn't do

  • It doesn't store the audio of your calls.
  • It doesn't show ads, doesn't track you across other apps or websites and doesn't sell or share your data.
  • It doesn't use your calls to train artificial intelligence models: Anthropic doesn't train on what it receives through its API, and Diana turns off Deepgram's training programme in every call.
  • It doesn't make automated decisions with legal effects on you.
  • It doesn't use your contacts, your location, your photos or anything on your iPhone apart from the microphone during calls and the date and time.

4. Who processes your data

Diana uses these services, which process your data on Diana's behalf and following Diana's instructions (processors):

  • Supabase (Supabase Pte. Ltd., Singapore): Diana's server, where your account, the memory, the summaries, the minutes and your consent are kept. The data is in Frankfurt (Germany).
  • Deepgram (Deepgram, Inc., United States): turns your voice into text and gives Diana her voice. Diana uses its European Union server, which your data doesn't leave, and turns off its training programme. Deepgram only uses the audio and text while processing each request and doesn't keep them afterwards. Because the iPhone connects directly to Deepgram, Deepgram sees the iPhone's internet address. It keeps technical usage data, without audio or text, for up to 90 days.
  • Anthropic (Anthropic Ireland, Limited): its Claude model writes what Diana says, the summaries and the memory. It stores what it receives in the United States and deletes it within 30 days, unless its safety systems flag it for a possible breach of its usage policy (then, up to 2 years) or the law requires otherwise.

Apple processes your data on its own behalf for "Sign in with Apple", the App Store and payments, under its own privacy policy.

5. Data outside the European Union

The text of your conversations (with what Diana remembers about you) reaches Anthropic, which stores it in the United States and may process it in other countries where it operates. Also, although the server data is in Germany, Supabase's support team and some of its providers may access it from outside the EU (for example, the United States or Singapore). In both cases the transfer is protected by the standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission, which are part of their data processing agreements. Deepgram is a US company, but your data is processed on its EU server.

6. Deleting your data

  • "Delete everything Diana knows about me" (Settings) immediately deletes Diana's memory and all your summaries. Diana will treat you as a new person. Your account, minutes and subscription stay.
  • "Delete my account" (Settings) deletes your account and everything linked to it on the server (memory, summaries, minutes, sessions and the record of your consent) and revokes Diana's access to your Apple account. Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription: you do that with Apple (iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions).
  • One exception: if you had a subscription, when you delete your account the following is kept, with nothing that says it was yours: that subscription's number at Apple (Apple does know whose it is), its plan, when it expires and what was used that week. It prevents getting extra minutes by deleting the account and restoring the purchase (legitimate interest in preventing abuse). It is deleted once it has been expired for at least a week.
  • What was already sent to Anthropic is deleted within its 30 days, and the server's technical logs and backups within a few days.

7. Your rights

At any time you can ask for:

  • access to your data and a copy (also in a format you can take to another service: portability);
  • correction of anything wrong (you can also tell Diana in a call);
  • deletion (with the buttons in Settings or by email);
  • restriction of, or objection to, its use;
  • to withdraw your consent with "Withdraw my consent" in Settings, as easily as you gave it. After that you can't call until you accept again. Withdrawing doesn't delete what is already stored (the delete buttons are for that) and doesn't affect what was done before. While you have a subscription, the app keeps sending it to the server for your minutes.

To ask, use "Email to request your data" in Settings: it opens an email to soporte.juanllfontes@icloud.com with your account code, because Diana doesn't know your email or your name. We reply within one month, and only to the same email address the request came from.

If you think we are not handling your data properly, you can complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (www.aepd.es) or the authority in your country.

8. 18 or older

Diana is only for people 18 or older, and you confirm it in the notice before your first call. If we learn that an account belongs to a minor, we delete it.

9. Is it required?

To talk to Diana you have to accept the privacy notice and allow the microphone: without your voice and without sending it to these services, Diana can't work. If you don't accept, you can't call.

10. Security

All connections are encrypted. On the server, each account can only read its own data, and the app can't even read Diana's memory. The Deepgram and Anthropic keys are never in the app.

11. Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top will be updated. If the change is important, we'll tell you in the app and, if needed, ask for your consent again.
